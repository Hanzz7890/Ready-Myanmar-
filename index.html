See where disasters happened, what caused them, and how they affected people and places.

28 March 2025

The central Myanmar earthquake

The Sagaing Fault ruptured near Mandalay. Mandalay, Sagaing, and Nay Pyi Taw were among the hardest-hit areas. The damage figures below come from UNICEF’s June 2025 update.

≈52,000

2 July 2020

The Hpakant landslide

A slope collapsed at the Wai Khar jade mine. Heavy rain can weaken slopes, and excavation can change their stability. People working or living below unstable slopes are especially exposed.

≥172
Miners died
20
Years of changes at the mine studied

Study published in 2018

Sinking ground in Yangon

Satellite measurements showed that parts of Yangon were slowly sinking. Researchers linked much of this movement to groundwater pumping. A sinkhole can form if water dissolves rock below the surface and the ground gives way.

4
Zones with marked differences in ground movement
>20
mm per year difference in vertical movement in those zones
Read the Yangon study

26 December 2004

The Indian Ocean tsunami

A major undersea earthquake off Sumatra moved the seafloor and sent waves across the Indian Ocean. In low-lying coastal communities, those waves can carry water far inland.

0.4–2.9
Metres of measured tsunami height
22
Sites surveyed along Myanmar’s coast
Read the coastal survey

September 2024

Floods after heavy monsoon rain

Heavy monsoon rain and the remnants of Typhoon Yagi caused widespread flooding. Low-lying areas and riverside communities are particularly exposed when water rises. These figures come from the UN’s 27 September 2024 report.

≈1,000,000
People affected

>360
